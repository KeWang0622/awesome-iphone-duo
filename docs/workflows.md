# Things to do with the fold

These recipes are suggestions assembled from developer descriptions and release notes checked on October 26, 2026. **This project has not tested them on a physical Duo.** The numbered steps propose a small, repeatable session; the final check helps you judge the result yourself. Follow each primary link for current availability and setup details.

## Send a card that reveals itself

**Outcome:** A greeting whose surprise follows the recipient's opening motion.

**Requires:** [Papelle](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/papelle/id6816144743) on both phones; a photo or short video.

1. Choose a design, add your media and write a personal message.
2. Preview it by opening slowly, pausing halfway, then closing.
3. Share through Messages, Mail or AirDrop.

**Check:** Have the recipient open the received card; they need the free app, but not your paid design collection.

## Mark a take for your next short film

**Outcome:** A visible and audible reference for matching picture with recorded sound.

**Requires:** [Clap](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/clap-film-slate/id6824625416), Pro access or its trial, camera and sound recorder.

1. Enter the scene and take, then hold the open slate toward the camera.
2. Close the phone to clap. Review the take on the cover and mark a keeper.

**Check:** Record a test and compare closure with the recorded clap before relying on the timing.

## Play liar's dice across one table

**Outcome:** A shared game with hidden information revealed by folding.

**Requires:** [Peekaboard](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/peekaboard-2-player-games/id6819004793), a friend; Bluff Cups trial or Full Pass.

1. Start Bluff Cups and lay the open phone between you.
2. Fold your half toward yourself to inspect your dice, then return it flat.
3. Bid on the whole table or challenge the previous bid.

**Check:** Before playing, verify that the other player cannot read your dice from their seat.

## Learn a tune with physical bellows

**Outcome:** A short melody you play while supplying its airflow through the hinge.

**Requires:** [Fold Accordion](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fold-accordion/id6815058989) and Duo.

1. Select Fold input and choose a song in Learn mode.
2. Follow the illuminated melody keys while opening and closing the phone.
3. Add the bass part once the melody feels comfortable.

**Check:** If folding does not change playback, check the input mode and whether Auto Bellows is enabled.

## Give a naval battle a physical trigger

**Outcome:** Confirm a selected target by folding the phone.

**Requires:** [FleetFold](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fleetfold/id6817450309); Full Fleet after the first three free battles.

1. Enable optional fold-to-fire in Settings, then start a solo match.
2. Place your fleet and select an enemy-grid target.
3. Fold to confirm the shot; inspect its result before choosing again.

**Check:** Try the ordinary Fire button if a fold fails, separating targeting from gesture recognition.

## Make an ink print to share

**Outcome:** A personally made blot and an illustrated keepsake.

**Requires:** [Inkblotter](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/inkblotter/id6819932681).

1. Add ink to one page using taps, pours or smears.
2. Close the phone to press it, then reopen to reveal the result.
3. Describe what you see and share the blot with its reading.

**Check:** Treat the Examiner's interpretation as fictional entertainment; evaluate the artwork rather than using its reading as a psychological assessment.

## Build a five-album listening session

**Outcome:** A small musical selection arranged like a physical CD changer.

**Requires:** [Penta](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/penta-cd-player-changer/id6806427066) and owned library albums, or Apple Music for catalog streaming.

1. Unfold an album's jewel case and load its disc into the changer.
2. Repeat for your selection, then prop Duo like a laptop: wallet above, tray below.

**Check:** Play one track before filling the carousel; catalog streaming needs an active Apple Music subscription.

## Experiment: record a robot-gripper demonstration

**Outcome:** A recorded gripper movement that can be replayed and exported.

**Requires:** [Knuckle source](https://github.com/keming-jiao/knuckle), its documented build environment, SO-101 gripper and Python bridge.

1. Follow the repository setup; begin with simulator input and the documented bridge.
2. Record a short open-close motion, replay it, then inspect the exported JSON.

**Check:** This is a hackathon prototype: physical-Duo testing was future work. Replay does not demonstrate machine learning.
